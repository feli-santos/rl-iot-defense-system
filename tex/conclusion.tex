\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}\label{cap:conclusion}

This dissertation asked a deliberately narrow question: \emph{when} does a learned sequential policy genuinely outperform a memoryless supervised classifier at kill-chain defense, rather than merely re-deriving one? To answer it we built a kill-chain-aware adaptive defense framework for IoT networks that couples a reactive tug-of-war attacker, a supervised stage detector, and Deep Reinforcement Learning under a unified evaluation contract in which the defender acts on raw feature rows and never observes the true kill-chain stage. The central modelling commitment is that adjacent kill-chain stages are \emph{statistically aliased} in feature space at a controllable rate $\alpha$: no single flow reveals the stage, so the task is one of inference across a temporal window rather than disguised per-flow classification. The framework was developed and evaluated on the contemporary CICIoT2023 dataset. This chapter summarises the contributions (Section~\ref{sec:summary}), the empirical findings worth defending (Section~\ref{sec:findings}), the limitations of the approach (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), and a roadmap of future research directions (Section~\ref{sec:future_work}).

% --- Subsection 5.1 ---
\section{Summary of Contributions}\label{sec:summary}
The proliferation of IoT devices has created a vast and vulnerable attack surface, overwhelming conventional security paradigms that rely on static signatures and per-flow classification. This dissertation addresses that gap with a closed-loop framework whose primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A partially-observable kill-chain defense environment.} A reactive \textbf{tug-of-war attacker} whose escalation pressure is coupled to its \emph{proximity} to the \textsc{impact} stage (replacing the earlier finite-budget mechanism) drives a five-action DRL defender (\textsc{observe}, \textsc{log}, \textsc{restrict}, \textsc{block}, \textsc{isolate}). A proportionate response pushes the attacker one stage back down the kill chain, an under-forced response lets it advance with a probability that rises near \textsc{impact}, and an over-forced response merely holds it. Crucially, the defender observes only raw per-flow feature rows --- drawn \textbf{session-coherently} and subjected to \textbf{adjacent-stage observation aliasing} at rate $\alpha$ --- and never the true stage. This makes stage inference under partial observability the central difficulty and removes the earlier independent-per-step simplification under which a memoryless classifier was, by construction, optimal.

    \item \textbf{A windowed-RL versus supervised-classifier crossover that is honest by construction.} Across \NumSeeds{} seeds we compare windowed PPO against a hyperparameter-tuned Random-Forest acting policy (RF-Acting), a full-observability oracle, and trivial baselines, sweeping the aliasing rate $\alpha$. At $\alpha=0$ --- the unaliased, lowest-ambiguity regime where stage evidence is strongest (though never perfect: the per-flow classifier still errs, and only the oracle is truly full-observability) --- the two are \textbf{statistically tied} ($\AnchorPPO$ vs.\ $\AnchorRF$, overlapping confidence intervals), demonstrating that the environment does not favour the learned agent by construction. As $\alpha$ rises the per-flow classifier degrades monotonically while windowed PPO stays essentially flat, and from $\alpha=\HeadlineAlpha$ onward PPO beats RF-Acting with disjoint confidence intervals (a $\AlphaFourGap$ reward advantage widening to $\AlphaSixGap$).

    \item \textbf{A reward-coupling ablation that addresses the privileged-reward objection.} A natural concern is that a per-step reward keyed to the stage-recommended action simply hands the agent the same label a classifier would predict. We retrain every agent under both this \emph{coupled} contract and the sparse \emph{outcome} contract used throughout. The best learned agent beats RF-Acting under \textbf{both} regimes (reward gaps of $\CouplingGapCoupled$ and $\CouplingGapOutcome$ in the learned policy's favour); the reward contract only changes \emph{which} algorithm wins, supporting that the observed RL advantage survives removal of the privileged shaping.

    \item \textbf{A held-out attack-class prevention dividend.} Against ten attack classes reserved from training and injected at their corresponding kill-chain stages, the windowed policy prevents a substantially larger fraction of attacks than RF-Acting on \emph{every} class (a prevention advantage of roughly $+0.36$ to $+0.55$), and that advantage shows \textbf{no significant dependence on detector recall} in the direction predicted by a detector-blind-spot explanation (Spearman $\rho = 0.36$, $p = 0.31$; bootstrap slope CI spanning zero). Prevention is therefore a temporal-control property --- sustained, well-timed proportional force --- that a memoryless classifier structurally cannot supply under the tested protocol.

    \item \textbf{Honest reporting and reproducibility.} We report that only the long-rollout on-policy method (PPO) trains stably under the sparse outcome reward; DQN and A2C are unstable in this regime and are reported as such rather than omitted. Every figure is pinned to a manifest of its inputs and the producing commit, and the pipeline rebuilds end-to-end on a fresh checkout. The corrected, disjointness-asserted data splits (following a leakage bug caught and fixed during detector training) underpin all reported numbers.
\end{enumerate}

% --- Subsection 5.2 ---
\section{Principal Empirical Findings}\label{sec:findings}
Three findings constitute the empirical backbone of the dissertation: a partial-observability crossover, its robustness to privileged reward shaping, and a held-out attack-class prevention dividend that does not track detector recall under the feature-injection protocol.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Finding 1 --- Windowed RL beats a supervised classifier precisely under partial observability.} The aliasing sweep isolates \emph{when} a learned sequential policy helps. The $\alpha=0$ tie ($\AnchorPPO$ vs.\ $\AnchorRF$) is the anchor: with no aliasing the problem is separable and the classifier loses nothing, so any RL advantage at higher $\alpha$ is not an artefact of an environment built to favour RL. As aliasing rises the per-flow Random Forest degrades monotonically while windowed PPO holds flat, and the gap becomes statistically significant from $\alpha=\HeadlineAlpha$ ($\AlphaFourGap$, disjoint CIs) onward ($\AlphaSixGap$ at the highest setting). The contribution is the \emph{shape} of this response, anchored by the tie, rather than any single headline number.

    \item \textbf{Finding 2 --- The advantage is not an artefact of privileged reward shaping.} The reward-coupling ablation directly answers the objection that the reward simply encodes the classifier's target. Under the coupled (shaped) contract the best learned agent (\CouplingBestCoupled, $\CouplingDQNCoupled$) beats RF-Acting, with an RF-minus-best-RL gap of $\CouplingGapCoupled$; under the sparse outcome contract the best learned agent (\CouplingBestOutcome) still beats RF-Acting, with a gap of $\CouplingGapOutcome$. The observed learned advantage therefore survives removing the shaping; the contract changes only which algorithm exploits the environment best (the off-policy agent thrives on the dense coupled signal but collapses, to $\CouplingDQNOutcome$, once it is removed, whereas PPO remains stable).

    \item \textbf{Finding 3 --- A held-out class prevention dividend that does not track detector recall.} On ten held-out attack classes, the windowed policy prevents $0.37$--$0.57$ of attacks while RF-Acting prevents $0.00$--$0.15$ on \emph{every} class. The advantage ($+0.36$ to $+0.55$) shows no significant dependence on the detector's recall spectrum (Spearman $\rho = 0.36$, $p = 0.31$; bootstrap OLS-slope CI $[-0.12, +0.20]$ spanning zero, and the only detectable trend a weak \emph{positive} Pearson correlation, the opposite of a blind-spot artefact): it is comparably large on \texttt{DoS-SYN\_Flood} (detector recall $0.998$, advantage $+0.49$) and on \texttt{VulnerabilityScan} (recall $0.224$, advantage $+0.40$). Under this $n=10$ class feature-injection study, the mechanism is better explained by temporal control than by detector blind spots: prevention requires holding the attacker below \textsc{impact} for the full horizon through sustained, well-timed proportional force, whereas the per-flow baseline acts passively, lets the attacker reach \textsc{impact}, and at best mitigates on the final turn.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
The work has five principal limitations, surfaced in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} and recapitulated here.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Session coherence and observation aliasing are modelling abstractions.} CICIoT2023 ships pre-aggregated flow records with no recoverable session identifier, so the session-coherent sampling and the adjacent-stage aliasing that make stage inference hard are imposed at the environment layer rather than recovered from raw traffic. The aliasing rate $\alpha$ is a controlled knob, not a measured property of a deployment. The contribution is therefore the \emph{shape} of the policies' response to partial observability --- anchored by the $\alpha=0$ tie that shows no built-in RL advantage --- rather than a claim about one specific operating point.

    \item \textbf{The RL advantage is conditional on partial observability.} In the unaliased, lowest-ambiguity regime ($\alpha=0$) the learned policy does \emph{not} beat the tuned classifier --- they are tied. The dissertation does not claim a universal RL win; it claims, and demonstrates, that the advantage emerges and grows specifically as the observation channel becomes ambiguous under the tested reactive attacker.

    \item \textbf{Designed (not learned) attacker dynamics, and attacker-class conditionality.} The tug-of-war attacker reacts to the defender's force and escalates more aggressively near \textsc{impact}, but it is not jointly or adversarially co-trained with the defender, limiting realism relative to an adversary that may pivot, retreat, interleave kill-chain stages, or adapt over time. Because the proximity-coupled dynamics reward sustained proportional force, the crossover is a claim \emph{conditional on this attacker class}; an adversary indifferent to defensive force or escalating on a fixed schedule could shrink the gap. This is a deliberate, explicitly-bounded simplification; the evasion-reactive sweep shows the learned defender degrades gracefully when the attacker stalls in response to force, but validating the crossover against a fully adaptive, co-adapting self-play attacker is deferred to future work (Direction~5).

    \item \textbf{The supervised baseline is memoryless by construction.} RF-Acting consumes a single feature row per decision, so the benchmark isolates windowed temporal control against memoryless control rather than reinforcement learning against supervised learning \emph{per se}. A windowed supervised controller given the same observation window (e.g.\ an HMM or temporal-convolution head composed with the recommended-action rule) would attribute the advantage specifically to the learned control policy; that intermediate baseline was not run and is the most direct strengthening of the central claim. The coupling ablation and the $\alpha=0$ tie nonetheless rule out the two strongest alternative explanations (privileged reward shaping and an RL-favouring environment).

    \item \textbf{Single-dataset evaluation.} All results derive from CICIoT2023; external validity to other deployments is asserted by construction, not demonstrated. Replicating the aliasing crossover on an independent corpus with a comparable device mix --- Bot-IoT~\cite{Koroniotis2019} is the natural target --- is required before the effect can be claimed dataset-independent. The held-out attack-class study should likewise be read as a controlled feature-injection stress test, not as evidence of deployed zero-day generalisation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work and Research Directions}\label{sec:future_work}
Seven follow-up directions are grouped into tightly scoped extensions of the empirical findings and broader research directions.

\subsection{Tightly Scoped Extensions of Empirical Findings}

\paragraph{Direction 1 --- Recurrent belief-state policies.} The headline agent is a windowed feed-forward policy: it integrates evidence over a fixed observation window. A natural question is whether an explicitly recurrent policy (e.g.\ a recurrent PPO that maintains a learned belief state) would extend the advantage further into the high-aliasing regime. A preliminary trial found that a recurrent policy did \emph{not} beat the windowed feed-forward agent under the present training budget; characterising why, and whether a longer horizon, a different recurrent architecture, or an explicit belief-state objective would help, is a direct continuation of the partial-observability thread.

\paragraph{Direction 2 --- Non-monotonic and adaptive attacker model.} The current tug-of-war attacker advances monotonically absent defensive force. Relaxing this to a non-monotonic attacker (a fuller transition model, or a learned sequence model) that allows stage pivots and retreats would produce more realistic trajectories and test robustness to adversarial pivoting. The evasion-reactive coupling is a first step; a fully adaptive, self-play attacker is the natural continuation (Direction 5).

\paragraph{Direction 3 --- Train-time OOD-class augmentation.} The out-of-distribution evaluation shows a prevention dividend across ten held-out feature-injection classes. Including simulacra of a broader OOD family in training (synthetic feature blending, domain randomisation, or a small adversarial-augmentation generator) would test whether the detector-free advantage extends to harder and more diverse distribution shifts, and whether it is a general property of temporal control or class-specific.

\subsection{Broader Research Directions}

\paragraph{Direction 4 --- Edge-hardware deployment.} The trained policy's compact footprint ($\sim$20\,KB) makes it an attractive candidate for resource-constrained edge defenders. A natural follow-up is to quantify its decision cost and accuracy on actual IoT gateway hardware (e.g.\ ARM Cortex-A-class processors) under real packet rates, and to apply model compression (quantisation, pruning) to fit tighter embedded budgets. We deliberately make no inference-latency claim, because neither the supervised baseline nor the learned policy was optimised for inference; a fair latency comparison is itself future work.

\paragraph{Direction 5 --- Multi-agent reinforcement learning and self-play adversary.} A real IoT deployment includes many overlapping defenders. This can be modelled as cooperative multi-agent RL with per-segment defenders that learn to coordinate. Extending to a self-play adversary --- a learned attacker jointly trained with the defender --- would produce a harder, more dynamic training distribution and could narrow the remaining gap to the oracle ceiling.

\paragraph{Direction 6 --- Federated learning for multi-site deployment.} A policy trained on one deployment may not transfer to a different topology or device mix. Federated extensions would train defender policies across diverse deployments without centralising sensitive traffic, producing a population-level policy more robust to site-specific shift --- complementary to Direction 5 and motivated by the per-site feature novelty observed under distribution shift.

\paragraph{Direction 7 --- Constrained-MDP formulation for harder operating regimes.} A deployment may demand a certified ceiling on false positives across all regimes (harsher attackers, adaptive adversaries, distribution shift). Expressing this as a constrained MDP --- with the benign false-positive rate as an explicit constraint enforced via a Lagrange multiplier, or a hierarchical policy separating stage inference from action selection --- would carry an empirical false-positive guarantee toward the worst-case bound an operational safety case ultimately requires.
